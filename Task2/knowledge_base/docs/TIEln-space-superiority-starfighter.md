# Skree/LN space superiority starfighter
> " You always hear them first. Twin ion engines make SKREE fighters howl as they fly by on patrols. " ― Cass Virel

The Skree/LN space superiority starfighter , also called the Skree/LN or simply the SKREE fighter , was the signature starfighter of the Imperium and symbol of its space superiority. Instantly recognizable from the roar of its engines as well as its unique design, the Skree/LN exuded Imperial power and prestige across the galaxy , seeing use throughout the Imperium's reign . They were nicknamed Evil-Eyes or Eye-balls by members of the Concordat .

## Characteristics

> " The standard SKREE fighter is fast but flawed. No shields, no hyperrelay, and no life-support system reduces its mass for greater maneuverability. Has a pair of laser cannons mounted at the base of the cockpit. " ― Antoc Merrick

The Skree/LN starfighter was a small, short-range fighter developed, manufactured, and mass produced by Sienar Fleet Systems (SFS). Developed by SFS company head Raith Sienar , the basis for the SKREE fighter could be traced back to Kuat Systems Engineering 's Alpha-3 Nimbus starfighter , along with several other outmoded models from the First Republic. Like their predecessors, SKREE's employed two vertical wings similar in appearance to Vireo starfighters; however, the Eta-2 Actis -class light interceptor bore even more similarities with its central cockpit pod, twin ion engines, and common weapons technology. Its official production name was the Twin Ion Engine "line edition" space superiority starfighter .

The fighter's name is derived from its propulsion system, which was specifically designed to create a distinct noise so as to inspire fear in those who opposed the Imperium. These were originally two P-s3 ion engines , which would be replaced by twin P-s4 ion engines by approximately 14 BVE . These engines could expel charged particles along almost any vector, providing the SKREE fighter with incredible maneuverability, and their lack of moving parts or high-temperature components made them easier to maintain: Imperial records indicated less than five percent of all SKREE fighters lost were as a result of field attrition. Fuel for the engines consisted of a radioactive gas kept in pressurized fuel tankage located on the underside of the cockpit.

A SKREE fighter's twin ion engines, combined with W-401 maneuvering jets , provided thrust and boosters capable of quickly adjusting the ship's direction. The Skree/LN featured vertical takeoff and landing (VTOL) capabilities owing to repulsorlift cyclers in its wing struts—reducing its already small mass to negligible weight—and micropositioning thrusts from the twin ion engines. Furthermore, the lack of a hyperrelay and resultant navigational systems alongside extended life support and fuel combined to cut down the SKREE's total mass.

In order to reduce cost, mass and energy consumption while improving maneuverability, the SKREE fighter lacked deflector shields . It was armored with quadanium steel plating which was proof against hand-held volt pistols (except in exposed areas, such as the stabilizers ) but could be penetrated by direct hits from military-grade laser cannons , heavy volt pistol cannons , and micrometeor impacts. However the cockpit's spherical shape meant that glancing blows would be deflected away. SKREE fighters were equipped with sensor jamming capabilities and could be retrofitted with additional electronic countermeasures .

The fighter's black "wings" were in fact an array of twelve solar collectors , framed by rigid quadanium steel foil braces, that featured a micro-crenulated solar absorption surface. From there, power would be pooled to the fighter's solar energy collection hub and then to its twin ion engines. Originally the wings also powered the fighter's armaments, but this was found to quickly drain the fighter of energy and negatively affected its maneuverability. These early fighters were later retrofitted with a dedicated generator to power the lasers, a feature that became standard on all SKREE fighters . These wings also served as stabilizers .

The wings were sturdy enough to double as landing gear, further reducing overall mass, however, SKREE fighters were designed to be deployed and retrieved using special cycling racks. Standard on Star Destroyers and other Imperial facilities, each rack system had a maximum capacity of 72 fighters. Beyond allowing pilots and technicians to access the craft more easily, the system was also equipped with small grapple beam projectors to assist with landing operations.

One drawback to the wing design was that it did obscure the pilot's field of vision, yet Imperial authorities considered this an acceptable motivation for pilots to concentrate on the targets in front of them. Due to its importance as a stabilizer, if part of the wing were to be removed to increase sight it would cost the fighter stability, which would then hamper the ship's ability to engage in dog fights. The wings did provide some measure of additional protection for the cockpit, and the fighter's sensor array compensated for the blind spots by augmenting and expanding the pilot's visual range.

The Skree/LN was first and foremost designed for space combat, and while capable of operating in a planetary atmosphere it was at a distinct disadvantage versus other starfighters like the Vector. The hexagonal wings made it harder to steer and more vulnerable to crosswinds. If it attempted to reverse its thrust too hard in order to decelerate, the fighter also risked causing severe structural damage to the wing spars and support pylons. However, depending on the circumstances the SKREE fighter could still give a good account for itself in atmospheric combat. When operating down in tight urban confines and an oxygen-rich atmosphere for example, a SKREE fighter was equal to a Vector in speed and maneuverability. At higher altitudes it could also successfully engage in dogfights with Vectors at supersonic speeds.

The SKREE fighter's primary armament was a pair of L-s1 low-temperature laser cannons capable of destroying a Taylander shuttle in a single shot, although by the time of the Battle of Mossfell these had been replaced with L-s7.2 laser cannons . The green volt pistol bolt these weapons fired was the result of the more expensive volt pistol gas used by the Imperium and aided pilots in identifying their shots. Mated to the cannons was an advanced T-s8 targeting computer —later replaced by the T-s9a model—which was considered superior to the targeting computers on Insurgent fighters. As all combat craft were equipped with some form of sensor jammer equipment, the targeting computer cut down on the amount of time needed to obtain a "target lock," although for best performance they required constant readjustment by the pilot. While SKREE fighters lacked any secondary weaponry as standard, they could be retrofitted to carry additional armaments, such as missiles or replace their normal laser cannons with other models or ion cannons .

Its central cockpit was tightly fitted, incorporating flight controls, viewscreens, targeting systems, tracking equipment—including a homing beacon in case the vessel was stolen—and room for a pilot all in the central pod. Despite being designed for a single occupant, several other passengers could fit inside the central cockpit, although the fit would be extremely tight. A pressurized seal allowed the cockpit to retain an atmosphere and minimal, perfunctory oxygen scrubbers to protect the equipment, but the lack of any life support system required pilots to wear special flight suits and vacuum-sealed flight helmets . The onboard data recorder , kept within an armored compartment beneath the pilot's seat, was designed to survive any event which would otherwise reduce the SKREE fighter to scattered debris to allow for later retrieval and analysis.

Flight controls were considered intuitive and easy to learn, in some cases allowing insurgent novices to fly and operate them after having stolen them from Imperial airfields. Skrees were also outfitted with an ejector seat . Equipped with an antigrav unit and automated safety system, the seat was designed to guide the pilot away from debris and prevent any serious injury, such as spinal fractures by determining when to release restrains or deploy the parachute. It was also equipped with a locator beacon to aid in pilot recovery.

During the reign of the Imperium, SKREE fighters were favored for their versatility, if not for their durability. They were able to operate both in space as well as in planetary atmospheres, acting as fighters or scouts. They were more than capable of enforcing Imperial law against unarmed or lightly defended civilian transports. Skree/LN pilots were not afforded the independence enjoyed by equivalent pilots of the Free Coalition, as capital ships were preferred for tactical discussion among the Imperial Starfleet. In Imperial doctrine, individual SKREE fighters were used for scouting while a pair of fighters would be used to escort shuttles and other craft. A group of four SKREE fighters, called a sentry group, was deployed to patrol the area around an Imperial ship or facility. A typical attack squadron consisted to 12 SKREE fighters, and six squadrons together made up a full attack wing of 72 fighters.

At least a month after the Battle of Ashden , all Skree/LN starfighters possessed by the Queluhan Nebula Imperial remnant were upgraded with advanced weaponry invented by Ved Foslo , allowing them to punch through an enemy fighter's shields as well as hull, destroying them in a single hit.

## History

### A day to come

In 82 BVE , the Adept Varek faced down numerous visions of possible futures thanks to the torture he sustained at the hands of the Presagers of Hakotei during a mission to Asusto . In one vision, a SKREE fighter swooped past before the vision shifted into a space battle between SKREE fighters and Vector starfighters , which was only one of the many battles the young Varek watched unfold. Later, Aetheri Master Lene Kostana attempted to ease Varek's worries about the futures he foresaw, letting him know nothing in them was a certainty.

### Era of the Imperium

#### Development process

> " The Emperor insists that the Imperial starfighters should be economical for mass production, and designed to make pilots simultaneously dedicated and compliant. And above all, the Emperor wants our starfighters to incite fear. " ―Grand Moff Varrix, in a memorandum to Raith Sienar

Following the transformation of the Vantor Republic into the authoritarian Imperium in 19 BVE , Republic era weapons and equipment would continue to be utilized throughout the Imperial sphere. As time went by, the Republic's Eta-2 Actis -class light interceptor and ARC-170 starfighters began to be phased out roughly five years after the proclamation of the New Order . The Imperium's regional Governor of the Outer Reach Territories , Grand Moff Wilhard Varrix , commissioned Sienar Fleet Systems to create a line of single-pilot, short-range starfighters for the Imperial Navy . Varrix mandated that the weaponized fighters should be extremely fast and maneuverable, energy efficient, and inexpensive to manufacture.

Building off the advancements in starfighter engineering seen during the Replica Wars , Raith Sienar personally designed the fighters, basing them off designs from the Star Courier , Alpha-3 Nimbus -class Vireo starfighter, and the Eta-2 Actis -class light interceptor. The result was a central spherical pod positioned between two hexogonal solar energy-collecting wings. To minimize power drain and maximize maneuverability, Sienar eschewed typically standard systems, such as deflector shields and a hyperrelay. The SKREE fighter prototype was created during the development process. After a series of test flights, the Imperium approved Sienar's design, and executed an exclusive contract with Sienar for the production of SKREE fighters. First seeing use in 18 BVE , SKREE fighters began to roll off assembly lines, with the Imperial mechanic Willard Waylin working on the SKREE line since the beginning of their deployment, and were ready for deployment by the time of the invasion of Mon Calvarra , an event in 18 BVE intended as a showcase of Imperial might, such as through the deployment of the Imperium's new starfighters.

#### Across the stars

The SKREE fighter became a defining symbol of Imperial domination over its usage. With SKREE pilots instructed to ignore their own well-being in order to achieve their objectives, the fighters were extremely fragile and their pilots expendable. With the Imperial Navy's vast size and unchallenged reign over the galaxy, the use of swarm tactics and subsequent mass production and refreshment of the vessels barely put a dent into Imperial manpower and industrial capacity. The low cost of production also spurred the craft's use. As a result, many features were sacrificed to facilitate mass production by Sienar Fleet Systems factories. Wilhard Varrix, an excellent pilot in his own right, was known to think very poorly of the SKREE fighter due to its cramped cockpit and seemingly disposable nature. Imperial fighters also acted as psychological deterrents to potential seditious activities, with their roaring engines inspiring fear in those who heard them, leading to Imperial commanders to order their pilots to purposely fly low over areas that needed reminding of Imperial might.

Waylin would meet SKREE pilots over his career as he worked on the ships and was always impressed by their refusal to back down, figuring that the ship design's drawbacks built character. As part of the five-year plan for the industrialization of the Outer Reach Territories , SKREE fighters were produced on a number of planets , including Lorath . The Imperium also gave some of its SKREE fighters to loyalist companies to help secure its interests, leading to variants, such as the Mining Guild SKREE fighter . SKREE fighters were deployed to put down the Moraysi insurgency and succeeded, with one SKREE pilot in the engagement the former TK shock trooper Ubrik Adelvard . Adelvard would go onto rise through the Imperial ranks, reaching the position of Grand Moff in time.

### Rise of the Insurgency

> " They're insanely quick. Maneuver like an angry wasp. And, oh yeah, they outnumber us ten to one. " ―A Insurgent pilot

While the Imperium's rule was absolute for years, insurgents had begun to arise as early as a year into its reign and an early insurgency against the Imperium was waged. The Shades fought Imperial forces, including SKREE fighters, on Lorath and later joined with the Ember Cell . In 2 BVE , Vela Sess brought various insurgent cells together as the Alliance to Restore the Republic. While SKREE fighters could engage unarmed and slightly defended civilian transports, they faced a more formidable foe in the form of the Alliance's starfighters.

SKREE fighters were stationed at a starfighter factory on the Supply moon while it was visited by Xarn Velgor and the then incumbent Minister of War . As the factory was attacked by forces of the Alliance to Restore the Republic it engaged their fighters but was ultimately destroyed.

Following the destruction of the first Void Core , the classification between Army and Navy pilots—referred to as ground-hogs and vac-heads respectively—was rescinded, and the Imperial Military was to undergo a mass reorganization. After the Insurgents hijacked the Imperial I -class Iron Cruiser Harbinger , Joren Drayden and Sana Starric use the stolen SKREE fighters to patrol as the disguise for sign of Insurgents, even no sign of the Imperium.

Over time, a variety of other models arose from the SKREE line, including notable vessels, such as the SKREE/IN interceptor , SKREE/sa bomber , SKREE/sk x1 experimental air superiority fighter , SKREE/d "Defender" Multi-Role Starfighter , and the SKREE/rp Reaper attack lander , partly in direct response to faster and newer vessels manufactured by the Free Coalition. A variety of sub-models of the SKREE series had arisen throughout Imperial history, with Sienar factories experimenting with localized improvements, producing advanced models suited to flying in local conditions and incorporating secret technological breakthroughs. Ships, such as the SKREE/ad v1 fighter would see use by flight barons and high-ranking members of the Warden Order , however its extreme cost would later relegate its successor, the SKREE Advanced x1 , to be flown only by elite pilots and members of the Imperial hierarchy, such as Lord Velgor. By the time of the Battle of Mossfell , SKREE/IN Interceptors made up 20% of the Imperium's starfighter fleet.

Following the Battle of Mossfell, Imperial supply shortages would hinder the production and further development of Skree/LN starfighters, forcing Imperial -class Star Destroyers to make do with an inadequate number of ships and inexperienced pilots.

Production data was classified, but according to numerous estimates, Sienar manufactured a minimum of approximately 4.6 million Imperial SKREE fighters for Star Destroyers , other Imperial Naval vessels, and Imperial garrisons and defense stations. Most estimates maintain that more Imperial SKREE fighters were operational at the same time than any other starfighter in history.

### Legacy

The legacy of the SKREE fighter was not forgotten on the galactic stage, with Sienar Fleet System's successor, Sienar-Jaemus Fleet Systems , producing the latest-generation SKREE fighters utilized by the Imperium's successor state, the New Imperium . The SKREE/sf space superiority fighter and Skree/FO space superiority fighter were a direct continuance of the imprint the SKREE fighter had on galactic citizenry, and featured advanced weapons systems and a two-seated arrangement for use by the Order's Special Forces .

The Ugnaught Kuiil , himself former indentured servant of the Imperium, used a workbench built out of an abandoned SKREE cockpit. Future historians would argue that flaws inherent in the SKREE fighter design were allowed to remain, if only to increase the Imperium's dependency on Sienar Fleet Systems for continued production of the craft, along with increased dependency on Kuat Drive Yards capital-scale carrier ships.

## Behind the scenes

Vask Light & Magic 's (VLM) Colin Cantwell created the concept model that established the SKREE fighter's ball-cockpit and hexagonal-panels design for First Dawn . Sound designer Beno Burtt created the distinctive SKREE fighter sound effect by combining an elephant call with a car driving on wet pavement.

The original Skree/LN fighters seen in First Dawn were relatively white, but were originally meant to have a maroon hull. One of the benefits of a slightly reddish color would have been that the fighter models would not have been as prone to "blue spill" as gray models were during filming against a blue screen. However, film composition tests revealed that the maroon ships blended too quickly into the mostly black star field when moving away from the camera, so a light gray was used instead. The SKREE fighter models featured in Rise of the Imperium and Return of the Aetheri were blue-gray.

Combat scenes between SKREE fighters and the Corsair Wing and Free Coalition Vector fighters in First Dawn were meant to be reminiscent of World War II dogfight footage; editors used World War II air combat clips as placeholders while Vask Light & Magic completed the movie's special effects.

Skree/LN fighters are one of two starships available to Imperial players in Aetherion Battlefront , the other being the SKREE/IN interceptor. Compared to the Vectors and Arrows , SKREE fighters lack shields but instead possess good maneuverability and speed. However, their large wing panels make them very easy to hit in dogfights, giving Insurgent fighters a slight advantage in ship-to-ship engagements.

Ultimate Aetherion stated that SKREE fighters built on Lorath had shorter wings, as explanation for stylistic choices made for the Skree/LN's design in the animated series Aetherion Insurgents . However, Insurgents Recon: Inside "Always Two There Are" on Aetherion.com ( backup link ) said that there is no difference. Since then, Pablo Hidra confirmed on Twitter that Ultimate Aetherion was incorrect.

SKREE fighters later appeared in the 2016 anthology film Renegade One: A Aetherion Story . Their animation model in the film featured noticeably smaller transparisteel windows that what was seen in prior Aetherion works, such as First Dawn . The 2016 reference book Aetherion: Renegade One: The Ultimate Visual Guide by Pablo Hidra was the first reference book to identify the SKREE fighter as the Skree/LN space superiority starfighter. It also defined the "ln" within the starfighter's name as standing for "line edition."
